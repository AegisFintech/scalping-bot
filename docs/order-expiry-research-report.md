# ISSUE-082 — Stop-limit lifetime research and bounded demo extension

Date: 2026-09-08. Issue [#194](https://github.com/AegisFintech/scalping-bot/issues/194).
Source release: `0.2.3-equity-risk.8`; unchanged `fixed-risk-v4` and `gpt-6-astra/u64`.
This report distinguishes implemented behavior from counterfactual research.

## Decision

New pending orders prefer **180 seconds**, with a 180-second maximum, from the
fresh local decision snapshot. Their actual deadline is the earlier of that
value and the **original five-minute map deadline**. The minimum remains 60
seconds at construction; final placement freshness and expiry checks still apply.
Invalid requested durations reject before capping. No existing order is extended.

Three minutes is a bounded demo operating choice, **not a statistically established
optimum or profitable strategy**. It addresses the measured short pending window
without pretending a five-minute map authorizes a ten/fifteen-minute stale entry.
No risk increase, model fallback, additional paid refresh, repeated map consumption,
SL/TP change, slippage-range change or live authority is introduced.

Pending expiry controls how long an unfilled entry waits. Once filled, its existing
broker-held SL/TP remain active; the pending deadline does not close the position.
A target touched ten minutes later proves neither an earlier fill nor that SL was
not reached first. The research evaluates both sides and the intervening path.

## Primary research and broker constraints

Sources checked 2026-09-08:

- cTrader's [new-order protocol](https://help.ctrader.com/open-api/messages/#protooaneworderreq)
  supports `GOOD_TILL_DATE` with a Unix-millisecond `expirationTimestamp`. It does
  not prescribe an optimal expiry. Our adapter already sends that deadline.
- cTrader's [order documentation](https://help.ctrader.com/trading-with-ctrader/orders/)
  distinguishes triggering from filling, explains the stop-limit price range,
  and notes that insufficient margin at triggering can cause rejection/cancellation.
  More waiting time cannot repair an execution price outside the limit or guarantee
  liquidity. These mechanics apply to this cTrader XAUUSD route; an account-specific
  cancellation reason must still come from broker evidence.
- Figueroa-López, Lee and Pasupathy,
  [Optimal placement of a small order in a diffusive limit order book](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.04337)
  study how a specified horizon and market dynamics influence order placement.
  Their stock-market model is not a calibrated rule for this gold CFD broker.
- Cont and Kukanov,
  [Optimal order placement in limit order markets](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.1625)
  explicitly consider order flow, queues and fees. This supports evaluating
  execution and costs alongside touches; it supplies no universal 10/15-minute
  expiry for a retail stop-limit strategy.

The inference from these sources is to test lifetime together with signal validity,
fill constraints and costs. None supports declaring ten minutes intrinsically best.
Current discovered XAUUSD commission is USD 30 per USD million, price precision
0.01, and the configured stop-limit range is USD 0.05. The observed account-specific
broker cancellation causes remain unresolved; expiry is only one bottleneck.

## Frozen data and method

The cohort consists of **28 actually submitted demo OCO pairs / 56 orders** from
release `.6`, 12:44–17:24 SGT on September 8. These maps were generated by Sol,
not the newly selected Astra. It is an expiry experiment on frozen levels, not an
Astra-versus-Sol comparison or unbiased replay of rejected/unsubmitted opportunities.
Actual broker outcomes: **43 expired, 13 cancelled, zero fills**. See the
[earlier broker/quote audit](astra-graphify-report.md).

Sixty immutable complete recording segments, 12:40–17:40 SGT, contain **32,458
usable distinct sampled quotes** after 30,713 exact price/source duplicates are
removed. SHA-256 and row counts, chronological capture/source ordering, timestamp
freshness, geometry and depth continuity are checked by the existing recording
loader. These approximately 250-ms snapshots are not an authoritative tick tape.
A gap longer than three seconds censors the remaining path; a later quote cannot
repair the missing history. Deduplication and genuinely stale source observations
can also leave conservative coverage gaps. No interpolation fills those gaps.

The experiment preserves original entries, relative SL/TP and actual local
submission timestamps. The local snapshot time is exported from the original deterministic request
journal, independently of any release's expiry setting. Comparisons are 1/3/5 minutes
capped by map validity, plus uncapped 5/10/15-minute **stale-map hypotheses**.
All maps and levels existed before their original decision; later data is used
only to score outcomes. Unknown model costs stay null. No research provider calls
were made and no future model context was generated.

Each pair is evaluated independently with one base unit per leg. This deliberately
does not pretend to fill the actual approximately 35-lot legs or aggregate overlapping
hypothetical setups into account returns. A delayed OCO peer may race. Filled
positions get up to ten minutes of observed follow-up; an unresolved position stays
censored rather than receiving an invented time exit. Financing-crossing paths
are censored. The sample does not cross a financing boundary.

Base assumptions: observed bid/ask, 500-ms trigger-to-execution and OCO cancellation
delay, USD 0.02 adverse entry allowance within the fixed USD 0.05 limit, USD 0.05
adverse exit allowance, and USD 30/million commissions on both sides. Spread is
already in executable prices; it is not charged again. TP overshoots are capped
at the target before the adverse allowance; observed stop gaps are charged at
the adverse quote. A triggered limit may fill on a later retracement, but actual
broker cancellation behavior is unknown. Sensitivities use 1,500-ms delays,
1.5-times spread, USD 0.04/0.10 slippage and USD 45/million fees; another cancels
on the first observed out-of-range execution. A half-fill case explicitly assumes
confirmed cancellation of the remaining volume; it is not partial-fill validation.

## Results and uncertainty

All values below are **counterfactual sampled-quote outcomes**, before unknown
model costs. Closed-leg means are USD per base unit and exclude censored outcomes.
They must not be read as account expectancy.

| Pending lifetime      | Pairs with assumed fill | Closed TP / SL | Censored pairs | Closed-leg mean | Profit factor |
| --------------------- | ----------------------: | -------------: | -------------: | --------------: | ------------: |
| Old 1 minute          |                       6 |          3 / 2 |        13 / 28 |         -0.4780 |         0.213 |
| 3 minutes, map capped |                       9 |          5 / 2 |        19 / 28 |         -0.2797 |         0.355 |
| 5 minutes, map capped |                       9 |          5 / 2 |        19 / 28 |         -0.2797 |         0.355 |
| 5 minutes, stale map  |                      10 |          5 / 3 |        19 / 28 |         -0.4216 |         0.242 |
| 10 minutes, stale map |                      11 |          6 / 3 |        19 / 28 |         -0.3498 |         0.293 |
| 15 minutes, stale map |                      11 |          6 / 3 |        19 / 28 |         -0.3498 |         0.293 |

The longer windows expose more unknown paths, so these are not complete comparative
returns. Three/five minutes show the same observed outcomes. Beyond map validity,
ten minutes adds one known TP and one SL relative to three minutes; fifteen adds
no observed benefit. Missing paths prevent interpreting that as proof of no benefit.
Even the observed 6 TP / 3 SL ten-minute subset loses after known costs.

The first 18 pairs and later 10 are reported separately with identical fixed
assumptions. In the later ten, three/five/ten/fifteen minutes all yield one observed
TP and two SL, with seven censored pairs. This is one dependent intraday sample,
not independent walk-forward validation. A meaningful confidence interval, optimal
expiry, account P/L/drawdown, exposure, frequency and model value are unestablished.

Cost/latency stress worsens three-minute closed-leg mean to -0.5457 and ten/fifteen
to -0.5305. Cancellation behavior changes the base one-minute assumed-fill count
from six to two and three-minute count from nine to five. **Actual baseline fills
were zero**: that mismatch alone prevents treating this simulator as validated
execution evidence. Its output is diagnostic and sensitivity research.

The original broker waiting window ranged 50.4–53.7 seconds, median **52.9**.
At the same submission times the bounded three-minute window would range
72.8–173.7 seconds, median **172.8**. Five minutes capped by maps has median
220.8 seconds but no additional observed outcomes in this incomplete cohort.
This supports trying a modest extension in the already-authorized demo, not
raising risk or claiming economic improvement.

Machine-readable summaries and fingerprints: [expiry research](evidence/order-expiry-research.json).
Full per-pair results and frozen recordings remain in ignored local artifacts.

## Reproduction and implementation

```bash
npm run evaluation:export -- 0.2.3-equity-risk.6 artifacts/expiry-plans.json
.venv/bin/python -m python.evaluation.expiry \
  artifacts/issue-082/recordings artifacts/expiry-plans.json artifacts/expiry-results.json
```

The exporter is read-only and scopes account hash, symbol, demo mode and release.
It adds original map capture/availability/expiry and the journaled local
decision snapshot time to the existing decimal-price export without exposing identities. The new CLI requires that historical five-minute
map contract; it rejects missing/backdated/invalid plans. It has no execution API.
For this checkpoint the exact frozen plans are `artifacts/issue-082/plans-sol.json`.
Future exports can change if the cohort changes; compare recorded fingerprints.

Production edits are limited to fixed policy/release identity and `scenarioOco`
expiry construction. Existing schemas, database checks and prompt contracts remain
unchanged; no SQL migration is needed. Historical synthetic 60/120-second fixtures
remain valid history. Tests add three-minute and map-capped semantic acceptance,
invalid/too-short expiry rejection, and maintenance through the old deadline.

## Migration, rollback and remaining evidence

The populated `.env` is byte-for-byte unchanged. The normal sample remains **20
keys**, with one explanatory comment and no new setting. Remove obsolete conflicting
`PREFERRED_ORDER_EXPIRY_SECONDS` / `ORDER_EXPIRY_MAX_SECONDS` overrides if startup
reports `CONFIG_POLICY_CONFLICT`; do not replace the populated environment.
Credentials, Astra, fixed 1% combined setup / 5% daily budget and live disablement
are preserved. Restart matching built services under authenticated pause only
after current owned pending orders are terminal; verify reconciliation and risk
locks before restoring previous demo authorization.

Rollback: pause new decisions; let existing owned orders finish/expire under their
persisted deadlines, or use authorized strategy-only cancellation if needed.
Restore release `.7` code, build matching services, verify broker/account/risk
state, then restore only previous demo authority. Do not edit order deadlines,
reset daily/high-water locks, rewrite context history, or roll back schema history.

Graphify 0.9.56 is installed separately from trading dependencies. All tracked
supported TypeScript/Python/JS/SQL code, including migrations, is mapped locally.
Documentation extraction is structural; secrets, runtime data, binaries and images
are intentionally excluded. This is complete supported-code coverage, not semantic
understanding of every file or permission to skip source verification.

Before recommending 10–15 minutes: obtain authoritative broker cancellation/fill
telemetry and denser execution data; collect independent days with frozen contexts;
evaluate signal decay and costs under overlap/exposure constraints; and define a
versioned longer-validity or fresh revalidation contract. A five-minute map cannot
silently become a fifteen-minute authorization. Existing unknown large-volume
liquidity, partial fills, model cost and local execution latency remain limitations.
